# Introduction & Framework Foundations

---

## 1.1 Digital Sovereignty

Securing digital sovereignty requires a comprehensive look at three foundational layers:

1. **Technological Autonomy:** Preventing vendor lock-in by enforcing open standards, transparent tech stacks, and seamless data portability.
2. **Operational Continuity:** Maintaining independent control over day-to-day operations, administrative access, incident recovery, and change management.
3. **Legal Immunity & Protection:** Ensuring full digital immunity and data protection against unauthorized or extraterritorial third-country access claims.

---

## 1.2 The SML Framework

The SML Framework establishes a rigorous audit standard for evaluating cloud and IT service sovereignty.

### Guiding Principles

* **Auditability:** Every assessment must be evidence-based, fully comprehensible, and reproducible by independent third parties.
* **Comparability:** Results must allow objective comparison between different service providers as well as across time horizons.
* **Proportionality:** Controls are tailored specifically to the relevant service type (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS, Managed Services, AI Services).

---

## 1.3 EU Cloud Sovereignty Framework (CSF) as Foundation

The ES³ SML Framework adopts and operationalizes the eight strategic sovereignty objectives established by the **EU Cloud Sovereignty Framework (CSF)**. 

To address rapid shifts in cloud-native technology and modern machine learning architectures, the ES³ standard expanded the original eight CSF objectives to incorporate a **ninth dimension: Artificial Intelligence (AI) Sovereignty**.

---

## 1.4 The Nine Sovereignty Dimensions

| ID | Dimension | Focus Area |
|---|---|---|
| **SML 1** | **Strategic Sovereignty** | Corporate governance, ownership transparency, and exit preparedness. |
| **SML 2** | **Legal & Jurisdictional** | Geographic processing boundaries and protection against extraterritorial access. |
| **SML 3** | **Data Sovereignty** | Access control, customer-managed encryption (BYOK), portability, and usage control. |
| **SML 4** | **Operational Sovereignty** | Administrative access separation, business continuity, and operational independence. |
| **SML 5** | **Supply Chain Sovereignty** | Subprocessor transparency, Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), and vendor risk flow-down. |
| **SML 6** | **Technology Sovereignty** | Open standards, cross-platform interoperability, and multi-cloud migration capability. |
| **SML 7** | **Security & Compliance** | Identity and access management, security visibility, and isolated sensitive operations. |
| **SML 8** | **Environmental Sustainability** | Resource autonomy, power grid resilience, and long-term environmental risk exposure. |
| **SML 9** | **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** | Model lineage, dataset protection, explainability metrics, and human oversight. |
